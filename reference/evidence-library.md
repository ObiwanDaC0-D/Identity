# Evidence Library — Version 7

This curated pilot separates **empirical studies**, **scholarly reviews**, and **research monographs**. It does not claim that all 28 frameworks have an equivalent experimental evidence base. The records below summarize publication abstracts and bibliographic information; they are not full-text systematic critical appraisals.

## Tajfel, Billig, Bundy & Flament (1971). Social categorization and intergroup behaviour.

- **Linked entry:** i2
- **Evidence type:** Experimental study
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.2420010202
- **Method:** Laboratory minimal-group experiments examining allocations between categorized groups.
- **Finding / contribution:** Intergroup discrimination appeared despite minimal categorization and no established history of conflict.
- **Limitations:** Artificial allocation tasks and selected participants constrain direct generalization to complex real-world discrimination.
- **Competing explanation / consideration:** Intergroup behavior is also shaped by competition, institutions, incentives and pre-existing social hierarchies.

## McAdams & McLean (2013). Narrative Identity.

- **Linked entry:** i6
- **Evidence type:** Scholarly review
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721413475622
- **Method:** Integrative review of narrative identity research, including adaptation and developmental studies.
- **Finding / contribution:** Redemptive meaning, agency and exploratory narratives are associated with psychological adaptation in the reviewed literature.
- **Limitations:** The review explicitly identifies unresolved causal direction and cross-cultural questions.
- **Competing explanation / consideration:** Well-being may shape life narratives as well as be shaped by them; broader cultural narratives constrain storytelling.

## McLean et al. (2020). The empirical structure of narrative identity: The initial Big Three.

- **Linked entry:** i6
- **Evidence type:** Empirical research
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1037/pspp0000247
- **Method:** Multi-investigator empirical analysis of the structure of narrative identity dimensions.
- **Finding / contribution:** Investigates whether major narrative dimensions can be organized into a parsimonious structure.
- **Limitations:** Structural findings depend on coding, sampling and narrative elicitation; do not imply a causal effect of storytelling.
- **Competing explanation / consideration:** Alternative models may emphasize culturally situated narrative features not fully represented by common dimensions.

## Burke & Stets (2023). Identity Theory: Revised and Expanded, chapter 8.

- **Linked entry:** i4
- **Evidence type:** Research monograph
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780197617182.003.0008
- **Method:** Theory synthesis of identity verification and nonverification processes.
- **Finding / contribution:** Specifies reflected, actual and self-appraisals and proposes coordinated emotional, cognitive and behavioral responses.
- **Limitations:** A theoretical synthesis is not a single independent experimental test of every proposed mechanism.
- **Competing explanation / consideration:** Situational constraints and social power may prevent restoration of identity verification.

## Stets & Burke (2025). Identity Nonverification, Emotions, and Cognitive and Behavioral Responses.

- **Linked entry:** i4
- **Evidence type:** Empirical study
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/01902725251318211
- **Method:** Analysis of a nonrandom national quota sample of adults reporting identity-verification difficulties during the COVID-19 pandemic.
- **Finding / contribution:** Reported identity nonverification was associated with negative emotions and coping responses consistent with the authors' hypotheses.
- **Limitations:** Nonrandom sampling and observational associations limit causal and population-wide conclusions.
- **Competing explanation / consideration:** Pandemic stressors and contextual conditions could contribute to reported emotions and coping.


## Appraisal checklist

1. Is the item primary empirical evidence, a review, a theoretical argument, or artistic pedagogy?
2. What population, sampling method, and setting were used?
3. Are constructs operationalized reliably and validly?
4. Does the design support causal inference or only association?
5. Are effect sizes, uncertainty, replication and competing explanations reported?
6. Does the evidence generalize across cultures, institutions and contexts?

## Scope

For acting pedagogies, avoid inventing peer-reviewed validation. Primary manuals and practitioner traditions are important historical sources but are not interchangeable with controlled outcome research. Additional studies should be added only after bibliographic and methodological verification.

## Additional curated sources (V7)

## Higgins, Bond, Klein & Strauman (1986). Self-discrepancies and emotional vulnerability.

- **Linked entry:** i7
- **Evidence type:** Experimental and correlational study
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.51.1.5
- **Method:** Two studies measured actual, ideal and ought self-attributes before later experimental sessions.
- **Finding / contribution:** Examined how discrepancy magnitude, accessibility and type relate to affective vulnerability.
- **Limitations:** Measurement and experimental context limit generalization; emotion-specific predictions require independent replication.
- **Competing explanation:** General negative affect and situational expectations may also explain some responses.

## Higgins (1987). Self-discrepancy: A theory relating self and affect.

- **Linked entry:** i7
- **Evidence type:** Theory paper
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.94.3.319
- **Method:** Theoretical integration of self-guides, discrepancy types and affective predictions, drawing on prior research.
- **Finding / contribution:** Proposes distinct vulnerabilities associated with actual–ideal and actual–ought discrepancies.
- **Limitations:** Theory paper is not itself a single new controlled test of every prediction.
- **Competing explanation:** Emotional patterns may depend on accessibility, context and other affective processes.

## Markus & Nurius (1986). Possible selves.

- **Linked entry:** i8
- **Evidence type:** Theory paper
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.41.9.954
- **Method:** Conceptual account of hoped-for, expected and feared future self-representations.
- **Finding / contribution:** Proposes possible selves as links between self-concept and motivation.
- **Limitations:** Conceptual contribution alone cannot establish intervention effectiveness.
- **Competing explanation:** Motivation may depend on feasibility, strategies and external opportunity.

## Burke & Stets (2023). Prominence and Salience Effects on Identity Nonverification.

- **Linked entry:** i4
- **Evidence type:** Empirical study
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/01902725231202255
- **Method:** Study of a national sample of adults experiencing identity nonverification during the COVID-19 pandemic.
- **Finding / contribution:** Investigates whether identity prominence and salience moderate emotional reactions to nonverification.
- **Limitations:** Context-specific observational responses do not establish universal causal effects.
- **Competing explanation:** Pandemic-related distress and differing identity contexts may influence reactions.

## Stets & Serpe (2013). Identity Theory.

- **Linked entry:** i4
- **Evidence type:** Research synthesis
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-007-6772-0_2
- **Method:** Handbook synthesis of identity theory, survey and laboratory approaches, and measurement.
- **Finding / contribution:** Reviews identity salience, verification, multiple identities and emotions as major research areas.
- **Limitations:** A scholarly review is not a single primary test and should not be counted as one.
- **Competing explanation:** Related interactionist and social identity models can account for overlapping observations.

## Evidence classification note

A theory paper, research synthesis, philosophical argument, and acting manual are not experimental tests. Bibliographic summaries here are not a systematic literature review. Source selection is illustrative, not exhaustive; a claim of research consensus requires more comprehensive assessment.
