# Acting Theories and Pedagogies

| School | Figure(s) | Training focus | Foundational text |
|---|---|---|---|
|Stanislavski System|Konstantin Stanislavski|Given circumstances, objectives, magic if, physical actions|An Actor's Work|
|Strasberg Method|Lee Strasberg|Relaxation, sense memory, affective memory|A Dream of Passion|
|Meisner Technique|Sanford Meisner|Repetition, listening, spontaneous response|Sanford Meisner on Acting|
|Adler Technique|Stella Adler|Imagination, research, circumstances|The Art of Acting|
|Michael Chekhov|Michael Chekhov|Psychological gesture, atmosphere, movement|To the Actor|
|Brechtian theatre|Bertolt Brecht|Gestus, historicization, critical distance|Brecht on Theatre|
|Practical Aesthetics|David Mamet; William H. Macy|Playable actions, essential action, as-if|A Practical Handbook for the Actor|
|Hagen Technique|Uta Hagen|Observation, substitution, truthful behavior|Respect for Acting|
|Viewpoints|Mary Overlie; Anne Bogart; Tina Landau|Spatial-temporal awareness, ensemble composition|The Viewpoints Book|
|Suzuki Method|Tadashi Suzuki|Physical discipline, grounding and focus|The Way of Acting|
|Lecoq|Jacques Lecoq|Physical theatre, mask, movement, clown|The Moving Body|
|Classical rhetorical acting|Quintilian and later traditions|Voice, gesture and expressive delivery|Institutio Oratoria XI|

## Comparative questions

What is the actor attending to: internal memory, partner, imagined circumstances, physical action, or audience? How is character motivation made playable? What is the role of the ensemble and social context?

**Cautions:** Strasberg's Method is not synonymous with all Stanislavskian acting. Stanislavski's approach evolved over time. Viewpoints draws on Mary Overlie's earlier work. No single pedagogy has been established as universally superior by controlled comparison.
