# Identity × Acting: Intersections and Research

| Identity lens | Acting connection | Shared question | Important limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mead's role-taking | Stanislavski / Adler | How can we imagine another standpoint? | Character rehearsal differs from social development. |
| Goffman's dramaturgy | Stage acting | How do audiences affect self-presentation? | Ordinary conduct need not be deception. |
| Narrative identity | Character biography | How do stories organize meaning? | Fictional biographies differ from autobiography. |
| Possible selves | Improvisation | Can imagined futures guide behavior? | Rehearsal alone cannot ensure lasting change. |
| Self-perception | Behavioral rehearsal | Can actions affect self-inference? | Context and perceived choice matter. |
| Embodied cognition | Chekhov / Suzuki / Lecoq | How do body and experience interact? | Specific causal effects need testing. |
| Social identity | Ensemble training | How does belonging shape behavior? | Transfer beyond the group is uncertain. |
| Structural identity | Brecht | How do power and institutions shape roles? | Critical theatre and empirical sociology differ. |

## Illustrative skill-development cycle

1. Specify a valued skill or role.
2. Observe its behaviors and social context.
3. Rehearse in a low-stakes setting.
4. Practice in real situations.
5. Reflect on feedback and personal authenticity.
6. Repeat and revise self-understanding cautiously.

This is a **conceptual educational framework**, not a validated therapy or guaranteed identity-transformation intervention.

## Research design prompts

- Distinguish immediate performance from durable identity change.
- Assess behavioral competence, self-efficacy, narrative change and external feedback separately.
- Compare intervention and control groups longitudinally where feasible.
- Account for culture, power, social opportunities and selection effects.

## Suggested reading sequence

1. Mead (1934) and Goffman (1959).
2. Tajfel & Turner (1979), Stryker (1980), Burke & Stets (2009).
3. Markus & Nurius (1986), McAdams (1993), Higgins (1987).
4. Stanislavski, *An Actor's Work*; Meisner & Longwell (1987); Adler (2000).
5. Chekhov, *To the Actor*; Brecht, *Brecht on Theatre*; Bogart & Landau, *The Viewpoints Book*.

## Glossary

**Role-taking:** Imagining another's perspective. **Identity verification:** Feedback consistent with an identity standard. **Identity salience:** Likelihood of invoking an identity across situations. **Given circumstances:** Facts of a dramatic situation. **Objective:** What a character seeks. **Psychological gesture:** Physical action used to explore character impulse. **Gestus:** A socially meaningful attitude or relationship in Brechtian performance.
